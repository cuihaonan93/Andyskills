# Reference-Paper Decomposition and Original Research Blueprint

Reference papers are evidence and design objects, not text templates. Extract reusable reasoning principles while keeping the user's research independent and traceable.

## Boundaries and routing

- Do not copy, closely paraphrase, translate-and-reuse, or imitate distinctive text, figures, data, code, conclusions, or visual design.
- Label source facts and your inferences separately. Locate material facts by page, section, figure/table, equation, or stable source segment.
- Do not infer full-paper conclusions from an abstract. State access and parsing limits.
- Use `nature-reader` for full reading, `nature-downloader` for lawful acquisition, and `nature-citation`/`nature-ref-verifier` for citations.
- Use `dashixiong-innovation` for broader gap discovery, `dashixiong-sci` after the author has verified methods/results, and `nature-polishing` for later language editing.
- Use the document Skill when a Word blueprint is requested; run academic scripts through the absolute `NatureSkills` interpreter and render-verify the artifact.

## Phase 1: Evidence decomposition

Inspect only user-identified papers. For each paper create six records, each with source fact, source location, interpretation, and relevance to the user's question:

| ID | Dimension | Required content |
|---|---|---|
| S001 | Problem and contribution | question, stated gap, claimed contribution, evidence supporting it |
| S002 | Method and validation chain | object, data, assumptions, workflow, baselines, metrics, validation, limitations |
| S003 | Argument structure | function of sections/paragraphs and the progression from evidence to bounded conclusions |
| S004 | Rhetorical function | define, contrast, qualify, report, interpret; describe function without reusing sentence frames |
| S005 | Figure/table function | input data, analytical role, claim supported, and an independent alternative for the user's study |
| S006 | Citation architecture | claim-source links, primary versus review sources, currency, correction/retraction checks |

For multiple papers, compare agreements and disagreements in conditions, assumptions, data, metrics, and conclusions. A difference is not automatically a gap or contribution.

## Phase 2: Independent research blueprint

Propose two to four candidate blueprints when the evidence permits. Each must state:

- research question and falsifiable hypotheses;
- relationship to prior work and independent increment;
- object, operating boundary, and exclusions;
- data/resources, method, assumptions, and provenance;
- fair baselines, validation, uncertainty, and failure criteria;
- evidence expected under competing explanations;
- feasibility, risks, negative-result value, and extrapolation limits.

Select or recommend a blueprint only after checking it against the user's actual resources and objective. Do not require ceremonial confirmation when the next action is low-risk and already implied by the request. Ask when ambiguity could materially change the research or overwrite/expose data.

## Phase 3: Paragraph cards

Build writing cards rather than prose that shadows a reference paper. Each card contains:

- paragraph objective and link to the research question;
- required author evidence, analysis, figure, or verified citation;
- logical order and terminology guidance;
- claim boundary and alternative explanation;
- source/provenance note and author verification status.

Use `[AUTHOR INPUT REQUIRED]`, `[ANALYSIS NOT RUN]`, and `[CITATION TO VERIFY]` visibly. Never let a placeholder look like a completed result.

## Deliverables

Default output: source map, six-dimensional decomposition, cross-paper comparison, candidate blueprint matrix, selected minimum validation design, paragraph cards, figure plan, citation audit, and unresolved questions. Word output is optional and must preserve these provenance markers.

## Acceptance check

- Source facts are locatable and separate from inference.
- The blueprint has an independent question and fair validation.
- Expected results are not prewritten as findings.
- Figures and text do not reproduce reference-paper expression or data.
- Every paragraph card declares its evidence input and claim limit.
- Copyright, citation, access, and reproducibility constraints are visible.
