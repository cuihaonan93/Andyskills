# Evidence-Preserving Revision Method

## Priority order

1. Correctness and research integrity.
2. Claim-evidence alignment and reproducibility.
3. Argument structure and reporting completeness.
4. Figures, tables, language, and formatting.

## Claim labels

- **O - Observation:** directly shown by data, analysis, or reported result.
- **I - Interpretation:** a reasoned explanation supported by evidence.
- **H - Hypothesis:** plausible but not established.
- **R - Recommendation:** work the author should perform or consider.

Never silently convert H or R into O.

## Diagnostic prompts

- What exact question does the study answer?
- Which result supports each abstract and conclusion claim?
- Are comparisons fair and based on declared baselines and metrics?
- Are uncertainty, variability, failure cases, and scope limits reported?
- Can a competent reader reproduce the workflow from the methods and supplement?
- Does each citation support the nearby proposition rather than merely concern the topic?
- Are causal or mechanistic terms justified by the design?

## Revision record

For each substantive change record: location, original intent, revision, reason, evidence source, and author confirmation required. Pure copyediting can be grouped.
